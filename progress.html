What the lab is measuring

Arsenic removal efficiencyHow much dissolved arsenic the attachment takes out.
Flow rateHow fast water drips through the full stack.
Filter longevityLitres treated before breakthrough. It sets how often the pouch is replaced.
Attachment durabilityHow well it holds up through repeated pouch changes and handling.

The commercial hollow fiber membrane is already validated, so testing targets only the PureHusk attachment.

Impact

One material loop, four kinds of benefit.

Health

Lower chronic arsenic exposure for households that now drink untreated groundwater. The exposure is cumulative, invisible and linked to cancer.

Environment

Arsenic is captured at the point of use inside a replaceable pouch, instead of staying in the water families drink. Spent media handling and disposal is a design consideration, carried forward from the weak sludge guidance documented for SONO.

Circular economy

Rice husk gains value as a locally produced filtration medium and is diverted from open burning. The feedstock is abundant, local, and currently a liability.

Access

Low cost and low maintenance, aimed at the communities existing solutions priced out.

Outlook

What is going for it, and what still has to be proven.

Going for it

  • Low projected cost.
  • Simple maintenance and high portability, with potentially higher adoption than incumbent filters.
  • A local, abundant supply of rice husk.
  • Room for local RHA and pouch manufacturing, and for NGO-led distribution.
  • Rice-husk waste turned into an economic resource, for a large and underserved rural population.

Still to prove

  • Field performance has not been established.
  • Controlled RHA processing is required and is not trivial. Quality control at scale is unsolved.
  • The media needs frequent replacement.
  • Groundwater chemistry varies between wells.
  • Commercialization has to meet BCSIR requirements.
  • Established alternatives such as SONO exist, and other safe-water infrastructure competes for investment.
