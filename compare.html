Why portable matters

Every established arsenic technology is household furniture. Portability changes who can use one: renters, people who move seasonally, and households without space or a fixed installation point.

Why it changes distribution

A small attachment ships and stocks very differently from a bucket system. That matters for NGO partners who have to move filters, and the replacement pouches, to rural households.

The real opening

It works. People still stop using it.

Field studies confirm SONO brings arsenic to safe levels, and it won major engineering recognition. Its documented weakness is adoption. In one survey, 28% of units were abandoned after breakage. Households cited cost, maintenance difficulty, weak sludge-disposal guidance and slow flow, and were reluctant to repair units themselves.

The same study found about 50% of households preferred a household filter and about 30% showed willingness to pay. It concluded there is a need for a low-cost household arsenic filter (Shafiquzzaman et al. 2009).

PureHusk aims at that opening. The target is low cost, low upkeep, and a pouch you replace instead of a unit you repair.
