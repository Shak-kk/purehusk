The problem

Two problems, one loop.

Bangladesh has arsenic in the water people drink and husk piled up in the fields. PureHusk is an attempt to use the second to fix the first.

Sheet 2 of 6 · The problem

Problem one

Most people drink groundwater. A lot of it carries arsenic.

~97%

of Bangladesh's population relies on groundwater for drinking and domestic use.

Euronews 2024 · Mongabay 2022

~46%

of tested groundwater exceeds the WHO arsenic guideline of 10 µg/L.

Ahmad et al. 2018

Where the arsenic comes from

  1. Himalayan-derived sediments built the Bengal Delta. They carry arsenic bound to the surfaces of iron oxyhydroxide minerals.

  2. In oxygen-poor aquifers, microbes reduce those iron minerals.

  3. The arsenic they held dissolves into the groundwater. Monsoon flooding and heavy recharge can mobilize more of it.

The contamination is geogenic, not industrial. There is no upstream polluter to shut down.

The aquifer itself is the source. Safe water has to be produced where it is drunk, which is the case for point-of-use treatment over remediation or regulation.

The health cost

Chronic and cumulative

Long-term arsenic exposure causes skin lesions and organ damage, and is associated with serious chronic disease including cancer. The water looks normal while the exposure builds.

The gap

Filters exist. Use falls off.

Household arsenic-treatment technologies exist. They run into cost, maintenance burden, slow flow and day-to-day usability. Those factors decide whether a device is still in use two years after it is handed out.

Problem two

Rice husk piles up, and burning it adds a second hazard.

3rd

largest rice producer in the world.

IRRI

~55 million

metric tons of rice a year in Bangladesh.

IRRI

~20%

of a rice grain by weight is husk, so husk is produced at enormous scale.

Husk is routinely dumped or burned in uncontrolled conditions. Processing conditions, not the husk itself, decide whether it becomes an airborne hazard or an arsenic adsorbent.

Open burning

A hazard

  • High-temperature open combustion releases particulate emissions.
  • It converts amorphous silica into crystalline forms.
  • Prolonged inhalation of respirable crystalline silica is associated with silicosis.

Controlled-temperature processing

A filter medium

  • The silica stays amorphous.
  • The ash is porous and has a high surface area, which is what adsorption needs.
  • One material loop tackles both problems: arsenic in water, and husk burned in the open.
